# Fernhill Astronomical Society: Sky Conditions Guide

## How we rate seeing
Seeing is scored 1 to 5 by the session leader at the start and end of the night. A 5 means stars are steady points and Jupiter's cloud bands are crisp at 200x. A 3 means moderate twinkling and planetary detail that comes and goes. A 1 means the image boils and high magnification is pointless. We record the score in the session log next to the date.

## How we rate transparency
Transparency is also scored 1 to 5, from how many faint stars are visible. We back it with a limiting magnitude estimate taken near the zenith using the Pleiades chart. Anything below 2/5 means we switch to bright targets only: planets, the Moon, double stars.

## Sky Quality Meter readings
Members with an SQM take one reading per site at 23:00 pointing at the zenith. Cobb Field averages 20.9 mag/arcsec2. Quarry Ridge averages 21.2 and is our darkest regular site. The Rooftop at the Library, used for public nights, averages 18.4 because of the town lights.

## When we call a night off
We cancel if cloud cover forecast exceeds 70 percent at 21:00, if wind gusts exceed 40 km/h (the Dobsonians act like sails), or if the temperature falls below minus 10 C. The decision is posted to the members' group by 18:00.
