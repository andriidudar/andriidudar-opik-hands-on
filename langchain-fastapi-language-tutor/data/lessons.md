# Señora Vega's lesson notes (fictional)

## Greetings and politeness
Use "hola" with friends and "buenos días / buenas tardes / buenas noches" with everyone else.
Ask "¿cómo estás?" to a friend and "¿cómo está usted?" to someone older or a stranger.
Reply with "bien, gracias, ¿y tú?" (informal) or "bien, gracias, ¿y usted?" (formal).
Vocabulary: por favor (please), gracias (thanks), de nada (you're welcome), perdón (sorry / excuse me).

## Ser vs estar
Ser is for identity and lasting traits: "Soy Ana", "Es alto", "Somos de Chile".
Estar is for location and states that change: "Estoy en casa", "Está cansado", "Estamos listos".
Memory trick: DOCTOR for ser (Description, Occupation, Characteristic, Time, Origin, Relationship),
PLACE for estar (Position, Location, Action, Condition, Emotion).

## Present tense -ar verbs
Drop -ar and add -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an. Example hablar: hablo, hablas, habla, hablamos, habláis, hablan.
Vocabulary: cantar (to sing), bailar (to dance), estudiar (to study), trabajar (to work), comprar (to buy).

## Ordering food
"Quisiera un café, por favor." is polite. "La cuenta, por favor" asks for the bill.
Vocabulary: el desayuno (breakfast), el almuerzo (lunch), la cena (dinner), la propina (tip), el menú (menu), agua (water).
In Spain lunch is around 2 pm and dinner after 9 pm.

## Numbers and prices
1 uno, 2 dos, 3 tres, 4 cuatro, 5 cinco, 6 seis, 7 siete, 8 ocho, 9 nueve, 10 diez.
Ask the price with "¿Cuánto cuesta?" (one item) or "¿Cuánto cuestan?" (several items).
